## What 666clouds VPS is, and where a cheap VPS stops working

Three things decide whether a server can hold a TikTok account or a ChatGPT session, and none of them is CPU count.

**IP type.** A datacenter IP is an address block registered to a hosting company, and OpenAI has been mass-blocking those blocks since 2023. A native IP is registered to a consumer ISP in the country it claims to sit in. A dual-ISP residential IP goes one step further: the range is announced by two ISPs and shows up in IP databases as home broadband. One overseas hosting review put it plainly after testing a Korean plan: the IP registers as household broadband, and TikTok, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Netflix all opened without trouble. That single property is what you are paying for.

**Return route.** The path your packets take back to China decides whether the connection survives 8pm. CN2 GIA is China Telecom's premium line, AS9929 is China Unicom's, AS4837 is Unicom's cheaper international line, and CMI is China Mobile's. A clean IP on a congested return path gives you a server that unlocks everything and still feels unusable at peak hours.

**Hardware.** Everyone selling this category cuts the same corner. One server review blog described 666clouds' mainstream spec as 1 core and 1 GB of RAM with 10–20 GB of SSD, and concluded that it is fine for running ChatGPT and TikTok operations but not worth considering for large applications or databases. Read that as accurate rather than dismissive. The CPU is a means of holding the IP online.

## Where 666clouds falls short, and who should not buy

Disk performance is throttled, and it is not a subtle throttle. Both independent reviews recorded identical FIO 4K numbers: 4.91 MB/s read, 4.94 MB/s write, 1.2K IOPS. Identical figures across two different machines in two different countries means a provider-side cap, and the US review said so directly, describing the current performance as strictly limited by the provider. Sequential I/O measured around 345–370 MB/s, so light web work is fine. Anything that leans on random reads and writes will crawl.

There is no IPv6. Neither review machine had it, which rules out a whole class of modern deployments and contradicts any guide that mentions sharing IPv6 addresses across accounts.

The UK line has a history. In late 2025 the official Telegram channel announced that the UK IPs could not be recovered, that a new IP channel was being negotiated, and that affected customers should open a ticket for a refund. UK stock has since been restocked, and the plans are on sale again. The episode still tells you something about how thin single-location IP supply can get.

Stock and routing move. The tracker that produced the table above explicitly warns that stock counts and routes change and that buyers should confirm test IPs, traffic limits, renewal pricing and refund terms before ordering. The founding year is also unclear, with public sources giving 2020 and 2022 in different places, so treat the company's operating history as short.

Do not buy from 666clouds if you need IPv6, if your workload depends on disk IOPS, if you want a big multi-core box for the money, or if you are shopping purely on price per gigabyte of RAM. Those are all cases where a mainstream datacenter host beats this by a wide margin. The one refund commitment I could verify applies to a specific Philippines launch promotion and should not be read as a sitewide policy.
